Four checks before you order

1 · Don't buy the REX-C100 "kit"

Amazon's REX-C100 + K-type + 40A SSR kits ship an SSR-40DA, which switches AC loads only. Your heater runs on 12 V DC, so a DA relay will never turn it on. Buy the controller on its own and a DD (DC→DC) relay.

2 · Model code must contain "V"

On REX-C100FK02-V*AN, the V means it drives an SSR. An M means a relay output. Check the listing title or photo before you pay.

3 · Measure your printed puller mount

Your parts are already printed, so the motor has to fit them. If Puller – motor mount has a round ~37 mm bore, buy the JGB37-520 7 RPM (default below). If it has a rectangular worm-gearbox pocket, it's the JGY-370 remix, so switch to the JGY-370 10 RPM. The Cutter – motor bracket should take a 12 × 10 mm N20.

4 · Microswitches, not tact buttons

The MS switch bracket and the plus/minus buttons press two 3-pin KW11-3Z microswitches (20 × 10 mm). Their NO/NC contacts reverse the blade-height motor, and 4-pin tact switches can't do that.

Sequence

Assembly, in this order

  1. Drill the nozzle to 1.8 mm

    • Start from the 1.2 mm Volcano nozzle (much easier than 0.4 mm). Clamp it in a vice, never in your hand.
    • Use a pin vise or a drill on its slowest speed with a drop of oil. Go through in 3–4 pecks and clear the brass chips each time.
    • Lightly chamfer the entry side so the strip folds smoothly. Keep the exit edge sharp.

  2. Build the hot end

    • Screw the nozzle into the V6 block. Slide the 40 W cartridge into the 6 mm bore and lock it with the block's grub/clamp screw.
    • Put the K-type bead in the 3 mm sensor hole, or under the M3 clamp screw, and hold it with Kapton. Good bead contact is what keeps the temperature steady.
    • Mount the block on your printed Thermocontroller part with M3 screws and a ≥5 mm air gap. PLA softens at about 60 °C, so the block must never touch plastic.

  3. Base, cutter and puller

    • Lay out the printed parts on a 450 × 140 mm ply or MDF board in a straight line: cutter → hot end → spool. Fix them with wood screws.
    • Cutter gearbox: two M8 × 35 bolts and M3 screws. Grease the worm with Vaseline, as the original build does.
    • Fit the N20 in the cutter motor bracket and the JGB37 in the puller mount. Snap two 9 mm blade segments into the holders.

  4. Mains wiring, then test with nothing connected

    • Wire IEC → SMPS → REX following drawing A. Close the cover before you plug in.
    • Switch on and measure SMPS V+ to V− with your multimeter. Expect 12.0 V; trim with the SMPS pot if it's off. The REX display should light up.

  5. Heater loop

    • Connect REX 4/5 → SSR input and the heater through the SSR. Set SV = 100 °C first.
    • The SSR LED should come on and the reading should rise. If it falls, swap the thermocouple leads. At 100 °C, confirm the block holds steady before going higher.

  6. Motors and switches

    • Wire drawing B. Turn the PWM knob to minimum, flip the rocker, and raise speed until the spool turns slowly in the winding direction.
    • Press + and − and confirm the blade moves both ways. Stop before the blade holder hits either end.

  7. Set the strip width

    • Measure the bottle wall (see Calculators) and set the blade height to the calculated width, usually about 7–8 mm. Check it with a ruler on a test cut.

  8. First pull

    • Prepare the bottle: remove the label, scrub off the glue with warm soapy water, and cut the bottom off flat. Dented bottles give uneven strips.
    • Cut 30 cm of strip by hand, taper the tip to a point, push it through the hot nozzle (215 °C) and tie it to the spool. Start the puller slowly.

REX-C100 setup

  • At power-up the display flashes the input type and range. Confirm K and 0–400.
  • Set SV with the arrow keys to 215.
  • Autotune: hold SET about 3 s to enter the menu, step with SET to AT, set it to 1 and exit. The AT LED blinks for 10–20 minutes while it learns the block. Run it with the block mounted and the fan in its final position.
  • Menus differ slightly between clones. If yours doesn't match, the sheet in the box wins.
  • Aim for ±2 °C at a steady state. If it swings ±10 °C, improve the bead contact and run AT again.

Safety

Mains

Every 230 V terminal goes inside a printed or ABS enclosure with no exposed screws. Earth the SMPS case and fuse the inlet. Unplug before touching anything.

Heat

The block runs at 215 °C. Never leave the machine unattended while heating. Keep a ≥5 mm air gap to printed parts, and switch off at the inlet when you finish.

Blades & fumes

Snap-off blades are razor-sharp, so fit them with pliers. Work in a ventilated room and keep the nozzle under 250 °C. PET that turns brown is degrading.

v2 upgrades & open questions

Upgrade path (beyond ₹5k)

  • 12 V 10 A SMPS: more headroom (+₹300).
  • ESP32 controller: replaces the PWM board and logs temperature and speed.
  • Inline diameter sensor (hall-effect or optical) driving the puller speed in a closed loop. This is what makes ±0.05 mm, sellable filament possible.
  • Stepper puller (NEMA17 + A4988) for constant line speed.
  • Drying: PET absorbs moisture, so dry the filament before printing.
